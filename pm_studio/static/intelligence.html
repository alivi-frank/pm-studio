Activity-weighted hours: each person's active day is worth the configured capacity, worklogs are booked where logged, and the rest is split across what they actually touched — commits, transitions, comments — not across what is merely assigned. Unattributed is effort the portfolio cannot place: unkeyed commits, unlinked tickets, unknown people.

What became of the hours. Every hour in the window went into a piece of work whose fate is observable now: realized (it reached done), in flight (open and still moving), stranded (stale, abandoned, removed, or never planned), or untraceable (no ticket, so unknowable). The unit is the hour, so a team that writes one epic and one that writes forty subtasks are measured alike. Tickets done stays as a secondary column.

Problems the ledger surfaces on its own: work the board thinks is moving but isn't, work that is moving but the board doesn't know, effort landing nowhere, unhealthy patterns. Confirm or dismiss — the rules learn from it, and the judge reads your verdicts as ground truth.

Independent judge

A read-only agent scores the whole picture — attribution, plausibility of the hours, precision of the findings — reviews each finding, and proposes calibration and data fixes. Nothing it proposes applies until someone accepts it; the loop closes through people. The self-assessment beside it is computed deterministically and always exists.
